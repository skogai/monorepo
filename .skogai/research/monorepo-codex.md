I’d use **GitHub Issues + pull requests + Actions for the workflow, and Markdown in Git for the knowledge base.** Give people and agents local search from day one. Add RAG as a layer over the same files. That gives SkogAI a base you can clone, search, test, and change with ordinary tools, even when the experimental stack is down.

**Where Notion fits.** I’d keep using it for the shareable weekly summary and planning pages. Give each kind of information one home. An Issue holds what needs doing, the PR shows what changed, and Markdown records how the system works or why a decision was made. The 2026 survey puts Notion at **18.6%** and Confluence at **55.2%** among respondents to its knowledge-base question. Those figures describe wiki usage. [Stack Overflow Developer Survey](https://survey.stackoverflow.co/2026/technology/data?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

The other standard pieces I’d include are a **README with a fresh-clone quickstart**, **a committed dependency lockfile**, and **a smoke test in CI**. The quickstart is the contract. A fresh clone should be enough to run the documented checks and find a known answer in the docs. For RAG, keep a few real SkogAI questions and check whether retrieval finds the right passages before judging the generated answers.

**First move:** Use one real SkogAI issue to take a small Markdown change through a PR and CI, then verify from a fresh clone that `git grep -n 'search term' -- docs/` finds it. That exercises the entire base workflow and gives you the first known answer for a future RAG test.
